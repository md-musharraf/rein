Privacy Policy

Rein: Reels Blocker & Focus — com.musharrafapps.rein

Last updated: 10 October 2026

The short version. Rein collects nothing. There are no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, and no servers belonging to us. Everything the app records about your screen time stays in storage on your phone and is deleted when you uninstall it.

Who this is from

Rein is an independently developed Android app that helps you limit your own use of short-form video feeds and other distracting apps. In this policy, "the app" means Rein and "we" means its developer.

What we do not collect

We do not collect, store on our systems, transmit to ourselves, sell, or share any of the following:

  • Names, email addresses, phone numbers, or any account identifiers
  • Screen contents, screenshots, or recordings
  • Browsing history, search terms, or the addresses of sites you visit (the adult filter checks a site's domain on the device, and keeps no record of it)
  • Messages, contacts, call logs, photos, or files
  • Location data
  • Advertising identifiers or device fingerprints
  • Payment or financial information

The app contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK, no advertising SDK, and no third-party tracking of any kind.

Accessibility Service

Rein uses Android's Accessibility Service API. This is the permission that lets the app do its core job, and it is the most sensitive thing the app asks for, so it is worth being precise about.

What it is used for. To recognise, on screen, when you have opened a short-form video feed — YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, and equivalents in other apps — and to count how many times you have scrolled within one. That recognition is what triggers the block overlay, the scroll limit, and the daily time budget.

What it looks at. The structure of the screen: the package name of the app in the foreground, the identifiers Android assigns to on-screen elements, and the shape of the view hierarchy. It uses these to answer one question — "is this a short-video feed?"

Websites, when the adult content filter is on. The service reads the address bar of your browser to get the domain of the page you are on, and compares it against the filter list on the device. The domain is not recorded, kept as history, or sent anywhere.

Closing floating windows. If a blocked feed is moved into a floating picture-in-picture window, the service performs a swipe gesture to dismiss that window. This is the only gesture it performs, and it gives the app no extra ability to read anything.

Payment apps. When a UPI or banking app is opened, any block screen is removed immediately, so a PIN or password screen is never covered.

What happens to it. Nothing is stored and nothing is transmitted. The screen data is examined in memory, produces a yes/no answer, and is discarded. The app does not keep a log of what was on your screen, and it has no way of sending one anywhere.

What is saved. Only counts and durations, per app, per day — for example "Instagram: 34 minutes, 210 scrolls today". These are written to a database on your phone so the app can draw your statistics and enforce your limits. They never leave the device.

Usage Access

The Usage Access permission tells the app which application is currently in the foreground and for how long. It is used to enforce the daily time budgets you set and to show your usage statistics. This information is read on the device, used on the device, and never transmitted.

Display over other apps

This permission is used only to draw Rein's own blocking screen on top of an app you have chosen to limit. It grants the app no ability to see what is underneath it.

Notifications

Notifications are used to show the state of a running focus session, to tell you when a session ends, and to warn you if protection has stopped running — the accessibility service can be shut down by the system's battery management without any warning, and a blocker that has silently stopped blocking should say so. All notification content is generated on the device.

VPN permission and the adult content filter

If — and only if — you switch on the optional adult content filter, Rein uses Android's VpnService API to run a local DNS filter on the device.

No traffic is sent to us or to any third party. This is not a conventional VPN. Nothing is proxied, routed, or tunnelled to a remote server. The VPN interface exists purely so the app can inspect DNS lookups locally and refuse to resolve domains that appear on the filter list. Your browsing is not read, recorded, logged, or forwarded.

The filter is switched on for new installs, but the VPN part of it never starts on its own: the app first explains what the filter does, and then Android shows its own permission dialog, which you can decline. Switching the filter off removes the VPN interface entirely.

The one network request the app makes

To keep the adult content filter useful, the app periodically downloads a publicly available list of domains from the open-source StevenBlack/hosts project on GitHub:

https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StevenBlack/hosts/master/alternates/porn-only/hosts

This is a one-way download of a public text file. The app sends no personal data, no identifier, and no information about you as part of it — the request carries only what any HTTP request unavoidably carries, such as your IP address, which is visible to GitHub as the host serving the file and is not received or retained by us. GitHub's handling of that request is covered by their own privacy policy. If the adult filter is switched off, this download does not run.

Apart from this, the app makes no network requests.

Where your data lives

Your settings, limits, schedules, and daily usage statistics are stored on your device using Android's DataStore and a local Room database. There is no cloud sync and no backup to our systems, because there are no systems of ours for them to go to. Uninstalling Rein deletes all of it.

If you use the app's CSV export feature, the resulting file is written to the app's own storage and shared only with the app you explicitly pick from the Android share sheet. We never receive it.

Children

Rein is intended for adults managing their own screen time. Because the app collects no personal information from anyone, it collects none from children either.

Your control

Every permission described above can be revoked at any time from Android's Settings, and the app will keep working with reduced capability rather than refusing to open. Some protections deliberately require a waiting period before they can be switched off inside the app — that delay is the feature you asked for, and it never applies to Android's own permission screens, which remain available to you immediately and unconditionally.

Changes

If this policy changes, the "last updated" date above changes with it. If a future version of the app ever begins collecting data, this policy will be updated before that version ships and the change will be described in the app's release notes.
